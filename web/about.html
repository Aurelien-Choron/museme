Music, by how it sounds.

Pick a track and Museme returns the ten tracks of its catalogue whose audio is closest — whatever the artist's fame or the tags attached to them.

How it works

  1. Listening. Each track's 30-second preview goes through CLAP (LAION's larger_clap_general), a model trained on pairs of sounds and text descriptions. It turns the preview into 512 numbers that describe how it sounds.
  2. Genre helps. The genre of the track's album on Deezer adds 17 numbers. Genres sit on a circle where neighbours count as half-similar, and Pop, close to everything, has its own axis. The genre block gets a weight of 4 against the 512 sound numbers: about half of the recommendations stay in the track's genre, the rest are close-sounding tracks from elsewhere.
  3. Comparing. Cosine similarity between the picked track and the whole catalogue; the ten closest are shown. It is computed once for every track, which is why the answer is instant.

Does it work?

Before choosing CLAP, the 2021 audio descriptors, corrected versions of them and CLAP were compared in a blind listening test: 20 tracks, 277 suggestions shuffled together, each rated without knowing which method made it.

2021 descriptors (librosa)17%28%55%
Corrected descriptors31%34%35%
CLAP63%26%11%

Two thirds of the tracks also find another song by the same artist among their recommendations, although the model never sees the artist.

The catalogue

4,724 tracks: 3,815 collected from Spotify in 2021, 909 added in 2026. Previews and covers are streamed from Spotify; genres come from Deezer's public API (Deezer's 16 genres, plus Country).

History

Museme started in 2021 as a team project at the Wild Code School, as a Dash app comparing hand-crafted audio descriptors. It was brought back to life in 2026: a larger catalogue, recommendations by CLAP, and this static site.
